## Overview

Crash frequency modeling is essential for identifying high-risk road segments and improving traffic safety. However, crash datasets are often plagued by **excessive zero observations** — most road segments record zero crashes — which undermines model accuracy.

Existing approaches (statistical models, resampling techniques) are limited by strict distributional assumptions or fail to handle the **multi-type nature of crash data** (count, ordinal, nominal, and real-valued variables). This project proposes a data-driven approach using **TabSyn**, a hybrid VAE-Diffusion generative neural network, to augment crash data while preserving inter-variable correlations.

## Approach

- Transformed multi-type crash variables into a unified continuous latent space through a **tokenizer-based embedding** and **VAE encoding**.
- Applied a **Diffusion model** in the latent space to generate high-fidelity synthetic crash records.
- Integrated the augmented data with **XGBoost** for crash frequency prediction.
- Compared against state-of-the-art generative models (CTGAN, TVAE, GReaT, StaSy) and statistical baselines (ZIP, GAM-Poisson).

## Key Findings

- TabSyn achieved the **best distributional and structural fidelity** among all benchmark generative models.
- The proposed framework achieved the **lowest prediction error**, particularly for **Top-K high-risk segment identification**.
- Results demonstrate that deep generative augmentation significantly outperforms traditional resampling and statistical methods for imbalanced crash data.
